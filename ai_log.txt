     Forecasting setup

     Item: Time unit
     Definition: Calendar day D in Europe/Kyiv, DST-aware (2026-03-29 has 23 h)
     ────────────────────────────────────────
     Item: Target y_D
     Definition: Mean, over a fixed set of regions, of the hours of day D during

     which at least one official alert of any level (oblast/raion/hromada) was
     active somewhere in that oblast. Overlapping intervals are merged first.
     The
     set is every region in the data except Luhansk and Crimea/Sevastopol (exact

     list from Step 1). Range 0–24.
     ────────────────────────────────────────
     Item: Forecast moment (origin)
     Definition: 00:00 Kyiv on day D, i.e. the moment day D−1 closes
     ────────────────────────────────────────
     Item: Horizon
     Definition: 1 day (all of day D). Evaluated one step ahead, walk-forward
     ────────────────────────────────────────
     Item: Allowed inputs
     Definition: y and per-oblast hours for days ≤ D−1; calendar facts about D
     (weekday), which are known in advance
     ────────────────────────────────────────
     Item: Forbidden
     Definition: Any record, or part of a record, at or after D 00:00;
     statistics
     fitted on validation or test data; any tuning on the test period

     Why this target (D2):
     - After Dec 2025, one oblast-wide alert became several raion alerts.
       Counting level=='oblast' rows therefore collapses, and counting all
       levels inflates. Neither count is comparable across the change.
     - "Any part of the oblast is under alert" is the closest match to the old
       oblast-wide alert, so the series should stay roughly comparable.
     - Step 2 checks this with a plot around Dec 2025.
     - If a large jump remains anyway, the model trains only on data after the
       change. That choice is made on validation, not on test.

     Steps 2–7

     2. Target (src/target.py, ~40 min)

     3. Split
